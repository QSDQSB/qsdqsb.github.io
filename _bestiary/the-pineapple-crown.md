<section data-bilingual-lang="EN" lang="en" markdown="1">

In a valley of the Dolomites lives an orange cat.

In geologists' reports, the valley is called **Val di Cenere**,「The Ash Valley」. The origin of the name is unknown, its record is scanty, and no one ever looked into it afterwards. The name appears in two or three archives, never officially adopted, never officially abandoned.

No one knows when the orange cat arrived. When shepherds passed through, it was already there. When surveyors came to draw the map, the cat had no place on it, but it sat on the map, its paw pressing down on the scale. It wears a pineapple. Not a hat, *a pineapple*, its green crown of leaves resembling that of some long-extinct dynasty. It insists the pineapple is its crown.

It says a seagull carried the pineapple all the way from the Mediterranean. _"But there aren't any pineapples in the Mediterranean either,"_ the surveyor thought to himself. _"Can a seagull even lift a pineapple?"_ The shepherd found the whole thing baffling. All the inhabitants of the valley were curious, but no one dared to ask. The more absurd something is, the less anyone wants to be the first to point it out.

It has a grey cloak, a lute, and a coffee cup that never stops steaming.

On the lute is a five-pointed star of unknown origin. When it plays, the flowers tilt their heads ever so slightly, as if listening, or as if politely withholding judgment. No one has ever seen it brew coffee, nor seen any beans. The cup just sits there, steaming, as if it came from a time older than this valley, and intends to keep steaming forever.

Once a traveller lost his way before the cat. The traveller asked, "Where does this road lead?"

The orange cat lifted its coffee cup, took a sip, and said: _All roads lead to the same place, but this one has a better view._

The traveller said: _Then I'll take this one._

The cat nodded, perhaps in approval, perhaps just eager to get rid of him.

The traveller later published a book called _Somewhere_. The book never mentions the orange cat, but on the flyleaf is inscribed: _"He offered me directions and a drink. I took the directions, and have been thirsty ever since."_ Never mind that the cat had never intended to share its coffee.

Every summer the valley blooms with daisies. The orange cat sits there, the pineapple trembling slightly in the wind, as if it were thinking, or perhaps the pineapple is thinking.

It never goes down the mountain. Some say it's because it belongs to the heights. Some say it's because it's lazy. Then a child said: _Probably because the coffee down there isn't good enough._ The answer left all the adults silent for a long time. They dimly sensed it was correct, but they didn't know how to live with that kind of correctness.

As for what the orange cat does up there?

It plays the lute. It drinks. It watches the snow-capped peaks, and the peaks watch it back. Sometimes it tightens its cloak a little; sometimes it loosens it.

Later, a linguist came specifically to study the name _Val di Cenere_. She climbed for four hours and finally found the orange cat. Out of breath, she asked: "Excuse me, why is this valley called Ash Valley?"

The cat looked down at its cup, then up at her, and said: _Because I pour coffee grounds on the ground every day._

The linguist paused for a moment, took out her notebook, and wrote: _Cause unknown._

Her dissertation later won an award. The citation said she had, with rare scholarly honesty, confronted the deepest epistemological dilemma of onomastics.

The orange cat did not receive an invitation, but it didn't matter. The award ceremony was down the mountain. And the coffee down there isn't good enough.

<section data-bilingual-lang="中" lang="zh" markdown="1">

多洛米蒂的一个山谷中，住着一只橘猫。

山谷在地质学家的报告里叫**Val di Cenere ——「灰烬谷」**。这个名字来源不明，记录潦草，后来也没有人追究。名字出现在两三份档案里，从未被官方采用，也从未被正式废弃。

没人知道那只橘猫是什么时候来的。牧羊人路过时，它已经在那里了；测量员来画地图时，地图上没有它的位置；但它坐在地图上，用爪子压住了比例尺。它戴着一顶菠萝。不是帽子，是菠萝，绿色的叶冠像某个已经灭绝的王朝的冠冕。它执意说菠萝是它的王冠。

它说菠萝是一只海鸥从地中海一路带过来的。「地中海好像也没菠萝呀？」测量员寻思。「海鸥带得动菠萝吗？」牧羊人觉得莫名其妙。山谷里的所有居民都很好奇，但没人敢提问。越是荒谬的事，越没有人想做第一个指出来的人。

它有一件灰色的披风，一把鲁特琴，和一个永远冒烟的咖啡杯。

鲁特琴上有一个五角星的纹样，哪里来的没人清楚。它弹琴的时候，花朵的方向会稍微偏一下，像是在听，又像在礼貌地表示不置可否。没有人见过它冲泡咖啡，也没有人见过豆子。杯子就在那里，冒烟，像是从比这个山谷更早的某个时间带来的，并且打算一直冒下去。

曾经有个旅行者在它面前迷了路。旅行者问它：「这条路通向哪里？」

橘猫捧起咖啡杯喝了一口，说：所有的路都通向同一个地方，但这条路的景色比较好。

旅行者说：那我还是走这条。

橘猫点了点头，有可能在赞许，有可能是想快点把这个人赶走。

旅行者后来出了一本书，叫《在某处》。书里没有提到橘猫，但扉页上题了一句话："He offered me directions and a drink, I took the directions, and have been thirsty ever since."虽然橘猫从来没打算分享它的咖啡。

山谷每年夏天会开一次雏菊。橘猫坐在那里，菠萝在风里微微抖动，像是它在思考，或者只是菠萝在思考。

它从来不下山。有人认为这是因为它属于高处；有人认为这是因为它懒；还有一个小孩说，大概是因为山下没有足够好的咖啡——这个答案让所有大人沉默了很久，因为他们隐隐觉得这是正确的，而他们不知道怎么跟这种正确共处。

至于橘猫在那里做什么？

它弹琴，它喝东西，它看雪峰，雪峰也看它。有时候它把披风裹紧一点，有时候它把披风放松一点。

后来有个语言学家专门来研究Val di Cenere这个名字，爬了四个小时的山，终于找到了橘猫。她气喘吁吁地问：「请问这个山谷为什么叫灰烬谷？」

橘猫低头看了看杯子，又抬头看了看她，说：因为我天天往地上倒咖啡渣。

语言学家沉默了一下，掏出笔记本，写下：_成因不明。_

她的论文后来获了奖。颁奖词说，她以罕见的学术诚实直面了命名学中最深刻的认识论困境。

那只橘猫没有收到邀请函，但这无所谓。颁奖典礼在山下，山下的咖啡不够好喝。

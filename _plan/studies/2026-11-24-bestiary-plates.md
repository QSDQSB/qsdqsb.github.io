# The Bestiary's plates: a language for its art

**Asked by the owner, 2026-11-24** (I006): "What we want is just a scalable design language for the
arts", with three references: a storybook picture of the orange cat (cream paper, the cat seated, in
full), an ink-and-wash sketch of Sassolungo over Alpe di Siusi, and a dark oil of the herdsman's camp
at dusk with the plate on its stone. **Status: the lead's draft.** Every rule below is a proposal until
the owner keeps it; the medium is the owner's call, and the first plate arrives as a choice.

## What the references showed, set in the pages

Each was put in place of the photographs on the built pages (the door's cards and a fable's hero),
in a browser only.

|---|---|---|---|
| Storybook (the cat) | A cream block in a black room: the card is the brightest thing on the screen, and the hero no longer falls into the page | Fixes what the stories withhold: green eyes, a face, a seated pose; adds a brooch; holds a **goblet**, where the story says a coffee cup | A portrait: the hero's 2.4:1 cuts off the crown and half the face |
| Ink and wash (the meadow) | Bright and busy; the cover line has to fight the hatching | Invents nothing, since no creature is drawn | Wide, so it crops well |
| Dark oil (the camp) | Falls into the page at its foot; reads as the house's own (chiaroscuro, one warm light, near-black) | The plate on its grey stone, the herdsman, Sassolungo at dusk: all from the text | Wide; but the plate sits where the title's line goes, under the scrim |

The oil is the one that belongs to this site. The other two would make the Bestiary a different
house. The storybook's warmth is real and worth keeping, in the subject (a crown, a cup, a plate),
not in the paper.

## The language

### 1. One medium for the whole book

**A dark alpine oil**, in the manner of late nineteenth-century mountain painting: broken, divided
strokes, the paint visible, distances in cool violet and blue, one warm light. Every plate in the
same medium, so the door reads as one book however many fables it holds. No second style for a
"special" fable.

### 2. Light

One warm light out of deep shadow (PRINCIPLES, The direction). The hour comes from the story when the
story gives one (01: the light of dawn cut by Sassolungo; dusk turns the rock pink), and otherwise
from the plate's own subject: alpenglow, a fire, the cup's steam. Never midday flatness.

### 3. Palette

The site's own inks, as paint:

- **Shadows** fall to near-black, so the foot of a plate meets the page (`#151515`) without a seam.
- **Mid-tones** muted: verdigris and moss for the meadow, lapis and smoke-violet for the sky and the
  far ranges, oxblood and rose for alpenglow.
- **One ivory**: the brightest thing in a plate is its subject (the plate, the steam, the snow).
- **No saturated block.** The orange cat is the one creature with a colour of its own, held at
  antique gold and ochre, never a cartoon orange.

### 4. The creature: at the archivist's distance

The narrator records; it does not lean in. So:

- A creature is **small in its landscape**: no taller than a fifth of the frame.
- **Never a face to the viewer, never a portrait.** Back, profile at a distance, or in shadow.
  The stories withhold a face ("Orange cat." "No distinguishing features."); a plate does too.
- People as the stories have them: the herdsman a figure by a fire, the linguist out of breath on a
  path. No faces.

### 5. Canon: nothing that a file does not hold

Everything in a plate is in the story's text, or in a character's or a place's canonical sections, or
is plain landscape. Objects exactly as written: a **coffee cup** that steams (not a goblet), a
**pineapple** worn as a crown (not a hat, not a crown of gold), a **white porcelain plate with a chip
on the rim** on a **grey stone**. No invented accessories (a brooch), no text, no signature. Where the
canon is silent (the cat's eyes, Val di Cenere's shape), the plate stays silent too: in shadow, or
out of frame.

### 6. What a plate shows

**The situation, never the last image.** Each fable ends on a picture the reader is meant to arrive
at (the bare stone; the ceremony down the mountain). A plate that showed it would spend the ending.
01's plate may hold the plate of milk on its stone in summer; never the bare stone of the third
year.

### 7. Composed for the site's shapes

One master per fable, **3200 × 1350 (about 2.4:1)**, the hero's shape on a desktop, with:

- **The subject in the central band**, so it survives the card's 3:2 and the phone hero's 4:3 (a
  focus point `[x, y]` in the front matter, as a voyage's cover has).
- **The lower-left quarter quiet and dark**: the title and its line live there, over the scrim.
- **The bottom sixth falling to near-black**: the seam into the page.

### 8. Where it stands

The plate takes the place the photograph holds now, on the card and the hero. The archive's
photographs are the real Dolomites; the plates are the errata. Until a fable has its plate, its
photograph stays, so the book is never empty.

In the front matter: `plate: { url, focus }` beside `cover`; the layouts prefer the plate. The
`_bestiary` contract warns on a fable with no plate; it does not fail.

### 9. How it scales

- **A brief for each plate**, written from the story's own sentences: subject, hour, the one light,
  the creature's distance, the objects and how the canon states them, what stays out of frame.
- **A fixed style block and a fixed list of refusals** for whatever makes the plate (a model, or a
  painter), and the first kept plate as the style reference for every later one.
- **A check before it is kept**: the canon list above, the three crops drawn over it (2.4:1, 3:2,
  4:3, and the title's quarter), and the eye's test (DESIGN-LANGUAGE, "What the eye turns away"). A
  generated surface that shows its making (a uniform noise over the sky, as the oil reference has at
  full width) is finished by hand or refused: "not an AI-generated showpiece".
- **Plates are pictures, so they live in R2**, never in git, and go up by the owner's word, as the
  photographs do.

### Later, and only if wanted: the mark

A second tier, if the door or an atlas (I005) asks for something smaller than a plate: one canonical
object per fable as a hairline engraving in brass (the crown and cup; the chipped plate), the site's
heraldry. Not needed for the plates to work.

## What the owner decides

1. The medium: the dark oil, or another (the storybook's paper and the sketch were set in the pages
   above and do not sit in this house).
2. The creature's distance (rule 4) and the plate showing the situation, not the end (rule 6).
3. Whether plates replace the photographs on the cards and heroes, or stand beside them.

Then the first two plates arrive as a choice of two or three, each built into the pages and shot at
both widths.
